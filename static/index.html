1. Empirical Sound-Speed Approximation

c(T, S, z) = 1449.2 + 4.6·T - 0.055·T² + 0.00029·T³ + (1.34 - 0.010·T)·(S - 35) + 0.016·z

Where c is sound speed in m/s, T is reconstructed temperature in °C, S is estimated salinity in PSU, and z is depth in meters. This transparent empirical formulation approximates seawater sound velocity across upper-ocean research conditions.

2. Input Specifications & Provenance

  • Temperature Inputs: Reconstructed by OceanEmbed at six standard levels: [0, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000] m.
  • Salinity Estimation: Vertical salinity profile estimated via climatology-constrained exponential anomaly decay: Ŝ(z) = S_clim(z) + (S_surface - S_clim(0)) · exp(-z / 75m). When real SSS is unavailable, a baseline surface value of 34.5 PSU is used.
  • Pressure Assumption: Hydrostatic pressure is approximated from geometric depth z in meters.

3. Sonic Layer Depth (SLD) Detection Rules

Evaluated strictly across upper-ocean depths [0, 50, 100, 200] m:

  • Local Maximum: Shallowest valid candidate k (50m or 100m) where c[k] ≥ c[k-1] and c[k] > c[k+1].
  • Monotonic Increase: If sound speed increases continuously from 0 to 200 m without a local maximum, SLD is assigned to 200 m (flagged as deepening beyond resolved window).
  • Monotonic Decrease: If sound speed decreases continuously from surface downward, SLD is unassigned (flagged as no resolved surface duct).
  • Resolution Note: SLD is reported as estimated at the nearest resolved depth level; no continuous-depth interpolation is claimed.

4. Known Scientific Limitations

  1. Reconstructed temperatures are available at only six standard vertical levels.
  2. Only 0, 50, 100, and 200 m are used for Sonic Layer Depth analysis.
  3. Salinity is surface-constrained or climatological; subsurface salinity profiles are not dynamically measured.
  4. No acoustic ray tracing or acoustic transmission loss modeling is performed.
  5. Not suitable for tactical sonar performance, surveillance, or operational detection use.
  6. SLD values are nearest-depth estimates, not continuous-depth retrievals.

📐 Scientific Methodology: How Was This Calculated Step-by-Step? (Click to Expand Physics & Mathematics)
Step 1: Input Surface Forcing (68 × 60 Grid)
Multi-satellite raster inputs: SST thermal skin (MODIS/VIIRS), altimeter sea level anomaly Δη (Sentinel-3/Jason-3), geostrophic surface currents (u, v), and scatterometer wind stress τ (ASCAT) on an equidistant 0.25° grid across 5.125°N–21.875°N, 80.125°E–94.875°E.
Step 2: Spatiotemporal Neural Forward Pass
Propagated through OceanEmbed Core-6 (Causal ConvGRU + 3D U-Net Decoder, 1.69M parameters): $$\hat{T}(x, y, z) = \bar{T}_{\text{clim}}(x, y, z) + \sigma_{\text{train}}(z) \cdot \Delta T_{\text{norm}}(x, y, z)$$ reconstructing temperatures at 6 standard depths: 0m, 50m, 100m, 200m, 500m, 1000m.
Step 3: Root-Finding for D26 Isotherm
Depth where temperature equals 26.0°C is calculated per grid column by linear interpolation between downward bounding discrete levels $z_a$ and $z_b$: $$D26 = z_a + (z_b - z_a) \cdot \frac{T(z_a) - 26.0}{T(z_a) - T(z_b)}$$
Step 4: Exact Quadrature for Cyclone Heat (TCHP)
Integrated via trapezoidal quadrature down to D26 using oceanographic constants ($\rho_0 = 1025\text{ kg/m}^3, C_p = 3993\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$): $$\text{TCHP} = 0.4092825 \int_0^{D26} \max(0, T(z) - 26) \, dz = 101.8\text{ kJ/cm}^2$$
Step 5: Epistemic Uncertainty Estimation
Evaluated with Monte Carlo Dropout ($N = 12$ stochastic passes with frozen BatchNorm) to quantify confidence bounds ($\sigma_{\text{MC}} \approx 0.048^\circ\text{C}$ surface to $0.118^\circ\text{C}$ at 50m).
Step 6: Hazard Perimeter Vector Polygonization
All 3,139 valid ocean grid cells with $\text{TCHP} \ge 80\text{ kJ/cm}^2$ are polygonized into standard GeoJSON features (694 polygons covering 22.1% of the Bay) for direct ingestion into NDMA GIS disaster operations.
