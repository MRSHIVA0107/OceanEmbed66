1 Executive Summary & Societal Problem Formulation

Satellite constellations observe the ocean's surface skin densely and frequently (Sea Surface Temperature SST, Sea Level Anomaly SLA). However, critical physical processes—including cyclone heat reservoirs, internal thermocline waves, and underwater acoustic ducting—occur strictly beneath the visible surface. Physical ocean observing platforms (robotic ARGO buoys, moored buoys, research vessels) provide high vertical fidelity but remain critically sparse: only ~30 active ARGO buoys exist across the 2.2 million km² Bay of Bengal, surfacing only once every 10 days. Chartering research vessels (ORV Sagar Nidhi) costs ₹15–25 Lakhs per day.

This creates an acute 1,000-meter subsurface blind spot directly endangering three national priorities:

1. Cyclone Rapid Escalation
SST alone is misleading: warm skin (29°C) that is shallow quickly upwells cold water, choking a storm; but deep warm pools (D26 > 80m) trigger catastrophic Rapid Intensification (RI) within 24–48h before landfall, leaving coastal populations unprepared.
2. Naval Sonar & Shadow Zones
Naval surface sonar depends on Sonic Layer Depth (SLD). Below SLD, sound rays bend downward into acoustic shadow zones where submarines navigate undetected. Patrol aircraft (P-8I, MQ-9B) waste ₹12L per flight dropping sonobuoys blindly.
3. In-Situ Observation Blind Spots
With 10-day sampling gaps, fast-moving mesoscale eddies and thermal barrier layers pass unmonitored. Ship cruises are planned blindly across uniform grids rather than targeting high-uncertainty thermodynamic hotspots.
